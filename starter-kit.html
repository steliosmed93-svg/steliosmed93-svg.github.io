How to use this kit

Six steps from idea to submission

Print it, save it as a PDF, or fill it in on paper. Work through the steps in order: most reviews that get rejected went wrong at Step 1 or 2, long before the statistics. Each step ends with a short checklist, so you always know whether you are ready to move on.

StepWhat you decide

6Reporting: the PRISMA 2020 pre-submission check
Pyro's tip. Keep a simple log from day one: date, database, exact search string and number of hits. You will need these numbers for your PRISMA flow diagram, and rebuilding them at the end is painful.
Step 1

Choose the right type of review

Choose by your question, not by what sounds impressive. A scoping review is not a "lighter" systematic review: it answers a different kind of question (Peters et al., 2020).

TypeAnswers questions like…Typical features
Systematic reviewDoes intervention X work for outcome Y? What is the prevalence of Z?Focused PICO question, registered protocol, full search, 2 independent reviewers, risk of bias assessment, PRISMA 2020 reporting.
Meta-analysisHow big is the effect when we combine all similar studies?A statistical step inside a systematic review. Needs studies that are similar enough to pool.
Scoping reviewWhat evidence exists on a topic? Where are the gaps?Broad PCC question, maps the evidence, usually no pooled effect, PRISMA-ScR reporting (Tricco et al., 2018).
Literature (narrative) reviewWhat is the story so far? What do key studies argue?Selected studies, flexible search, expert interpretation, no protocol required.

Quick decision

☐My question asks whether something works or how big an effect is: systematic review (with or without meta-analysis).
☐My question asks what exists or how a topic has been studied: scoping review.
☐I want to explain or argue a position using key studies: literature review.
☐I have checked with my supervisor or target journal which type they expect.
Step 2

Build your research question

A good question names every element, so that a colleague could search for it without asking you anything. Fill in the framework that fits your review type.

PICO: for systematic reviews of interventions

P · PopulationWho? e.g. women with gestational diabetes
I · InterventionWhat is tested? e.g. walking after meals
C · ComparisonAgainst what? e.g. usual care
O · OutcomeWhat changes? e.g. post-meal glucose, need for insulin

PCC: for scoping reviews

P · Populatione.g. university students
C · ConceptWhat you want to map, e.g. fertility awareness
C · ContextSetting or place, e.g. Europe

PEO: for risk factors and associations

P · Populatione.g. adults
E · Exposuree.g. night-shift work
O · Outcomee.g. type 2 diabetes
My question in ONE sentenceEnds with a question mark. One population, one intervention or exposure, one or two main outcomes.
Step 3

Is it worth answering? The FINER test

Before investing months, run your question through the FINER criteria (Hulley et al., 2013). If any answer is "no", refine the question now.

CriterionAsk yourself
☐FeasibleDo enough primary studies exist? Do I have the time, team and database access?
☐InterestingDoes it interest me and my field? Will I still care in 6 to 12 months?
☐NovelIs there no recent or ongoing review on exactly this? (Check PROSPERO, OSF, Cochrane, PubMed.)
☐EthicalDoes it respect patients and data? (Reviews of published data rarely need ethics approval: check locally.)
☐RelevantWould the answer change practice, policy or future research?

Has it already been done?

☐Searched PROSPERO for registered reviews on my topic.
☐Searched OSF Registries.
☐Searched the Cochrane Library and PubMed with "systematic review" or "meta-analysis" filters.
☐If a similar review exists: I have a new angle (population, setting, outcome, newer studies, different design).
Pyro's tip. A review published 5 or more years ago, with many new trials since, is a perfectly good reason for an update. Say so clearly in your introduction.
Step 4

Plan your search strategy

Turn each element of your question into a concept. Within a concept, combine synonyms with OR. Between concepts, combine with AND. Use both subject headings (such as MeSH) and free-text words.

Concept 1 (Population)Concept 2 (Intervention / Exposure)Concept 3 (Outcome, optional)
Subject headings (MeSH / Emtree)
Free-text synonyms
Spelling variants and truncation (e.g. random*)

Example: ("Diabetes, Gestational"[MeSH] OR "gestational diabetes") AND (walking OR "physical activity" OR exercise) AND (postprandial OR glucose)

Step 5

Protocol, screening and risk of bias

Protocol and registration

☐Protocol written before screening starts (question, eligibility criteria, search, methods).
☐Registered on PROSPERO (systematic reviews with a health outcome) or OSF (scoping and other reviews).
☐Registration number noted for the methods section.

Screening

☐Two reviewers screen titles and abstracts independently.
☐Two reviewers assess full texts independently.
☐Disagreements resolved by discussion or a third reviewer.
☐Reasons for every full-text exclusion recorded.
☐A screening tool chosen (for example Rayyan or Covidence) and a pilot round done on 20 to 50 records.

Pick a risk of bias tool

Study designCommon tool
Randomised controlled trialsRoB 2 (Sterne et al., 2019)
Non-randomised studies of interventionsROBINS-I (Sterne et al., 2016)
Cohort, case-control, cross-sectional and other designsJBI critical appraisal checklists, or another validated design-specific tool
Scoping reviewsCritical appraisal is optional and usually not done (Peters et al., 2020)
Pyro's tip. Do not delete high-risk studies to make the result look cleaner. Keep them, and run a sensitivity analysis without them. If the answer changes, your conclusion is less certain.
Step 6

Before you submit: PRISMA 2020 check

This is a short pre-submission check based on the PRISMA 2020 statement (Page et al., 2021). Always complete the full official checklist and submit it with your manuscript; for scoping reviews use PRISMA-ScR (Tricco et al., 2018).

☐Title identifies the report as a systematic review (and meta-analysis, if done).
☐Abstract follows the PRISMA 2020 for Abstracts structure.
☐Introduction states the rationale and an explicit objective or question.
☐Eligibility criteria and how studies were grouped are clearly reported.
☐Information sources listed with the date each was last searched.
☐Full search strategy for at least one database is provided (usually as a supplement).
☐Selection and data collection methods: number of reviewers, independence, tools.
☐Risk of bias methods and tool named; results reported per study.
☐Synthesis methods described (effect measures, model, heterogeneity, sensitivity analyses).
☐PRISMA flow diagram with numbers at every stage and reasons for full-text exclusions.
☐Certainty of evidence assessed (for example with GRADE), if appropriate.
☐Discussion covers limitations of the evidence and of the review process.
☐Registration number, protocol access, funding and competing interests reported.
☐Data and code availability statement included.

The 5 mistakes that cause most rejections

MistakeFix
No registered protocolRegister before screening; report the number.
Only one database searchedUse at least 2 to 3, plus registries.
One person screened everythingTwo independent reviewers at both stages.
Vague outcomes ("effectiveness")Name the exact outcome and how it was measured.
Pooling studies that are too differentCheck clinical and statistical heterogeneity first; use narrative synthesis if needed.
